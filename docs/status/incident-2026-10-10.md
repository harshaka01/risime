## 2. Reading so far (root cause in progress)
- **The server process did not hang** in the captured window: every request was answered fast, the pool,
  Oban and Cassandra were idle, and local `/health` never slowed.
- **The network path to our two public addresses stalls.** New TCP connections to 203.115.26.139 (RisiMe via
  the edge) and 203.189.69.77 (Keycloak) sometimes don't complete for 6–8 s (connect time 0 = no SYN-ACK).
  General internet hosts don't show it. SSH sessions on :22 stay up because they are already
  established; only **new** connections stall.
- **Why the app shows "connecting":** the phone opens a new websocket and, every ~5 min, refreshes
  its Keycloak access token at risicloud.ai. Either one can hit the 6–8 s stall. A stalled refresh
  leaves the phone with an expired token, which gets `401 invalid_token`, and it stays on "connecting"
  until a refresh gets through. This fits the 401 bursts.
- **Still to rule in or out:** whether the stall happens at the edge router/NAT (both addresses go
  through it) or at Keycloak's host; Cassandra swap; a Risi call blocking a channel (needs the BEAM
  shell); Caddy websocket idle timeouts (needs the access log).

## 4. Root cause (step 2, 08:00 UTC)
**The office network path through the FortiGate (10.20.20.1, Fortinet MAC) was degraded from about 02:50 to
05:30 UTC.** That covers about an hour before and after the full outage of 03:50–04:30 UTC (09:20–10:00
Sri Lanka). The RisiMe server process stayed healthy throughout.

Evidence:
- **Both slow addresses are FortiGate virtual IPs for hosts on our own LAN.** risime.risicloud.ai
  (203.115.26.139) is spark2 itself, through hairpin NAT. Keycloak, risicloud.ai (203.189.69.77), is
  **spark1 = 10.20.20.14**, a LAN neighbour of spark2 that answers directly in < 1 ms. Everything a phone
  needs (the websocket, REST, the Keycloak token refresh) passes through these two VIPs.
- **The server's 10-min Keycloak key refresh timed out (5 s)** at 02:54, 03:04, 03:14, 03:24, 03:34, 03:44,
  04:04, 04:14, 04:24, 04:54, 05:04, 05:14 and 05:24 (13 times). It succeeded at 05:34 and 05:44, before
  the restart, and has not failed since. The only other failures on record are 2 on 2026-10-08 (08:10, 08:40).
- **05:41–05:50 probes:** new TCP connections to both VIPs sometimes never got a SYN-ACK (6–8 s, 3/60 and
  1/60 failed). Google/Cloudflare had no failures. Consistent with the tail of the bad window.
- **Since 07:40 UTC (60-min probe, result appended when done):** a 4-way probe every 3 s (both VIPs, Keycloak over the LAN, Google)
  shows 0 failures. VIP connects take 0.3–0.5 ms (hairpin on the FortiGate).
- **Idle-connection test:** keep-alive connections to both VIPs and to the LAN path are closed cleanly
  (FIN, an immediate error) after 3–5.5 min idle, never silently. So a stale pooled connection isn't the
  cause, and the timeouts were real network stalls.
- **Ruled out at capture:** memory pressure (23 GiB free; swap 8.2 GiB used but no swapping, si/so 0),
  Cassandra GC (max 499 ms, no blocked pools), the DB pool / Oban (idle, nothing waiting), DNS (8 ms),
  Caddy errors (none in its journal). Not checked: a Risi call blocking a channel (no BEAM shell: decision 075
  adds one) and Caddy websocket idle timeouts (its access log needs root; Phoenix heartbeats every 30 s
  keep websockets from going idle anyway).

**Why the phones showed "connecting":** a phone opens new connections to both VIPs all the time: the
websocket after any network change, REST calls, and a Keycloak refresh before each access token expires.
In the bad window those connections stalled or failed. A failed refresh leaves an expired token, so the
next requests get `401 invalid_token` (108 in 3.5 h from 7 phones). The app keeps showing "connecting" until
a refresh and a websocket connect both get through.

## 5. Fixes
1. **Network (Harsha / IT):** read the FortiGate logs for 02:45–05:35 UTC (08:15–11:05 Sri Lanka) on 2026-10-10
   and 08:05–08:45 UTC on 2026-10-08: session-table usage, VIP / hairpin policy hits, IPS or DoS-policy drops,
   HA failover, WAN link flaps. The full outage at 03:50 sits in the middle of that window.
2. **Detection (done / in progress):** the watchdog alerts `public_down` after 3 failed public checks
   (90 s). The monitoring stack (step 4) probes both VIPs and the LAN path every 15 s and alerts on
   connect time > 3 s, so the next episode has exact times for IT.
3. **Server:** fetch Keycloak's keys over the LAN and retry transient failures:
   - `sudo sh -c 'echo "10.20.20.14 risicloud.ai" >> /etc/hosts'` on spark2 (Harsha). Only spark2's own
     lookups change, and the certificate still matches.
   - The JWKS fetch retries a timeout once after 1 s (server role).
4. **Android:** shorter connect timeouts with a quick retry for the Keycloak refresh and the websocket, so a
   stall costs seconds instead of a "connecting" episode. Refresh well before expiry, and retry a failed
   refresh within 5 s instead of waiting for a 401 (android role, brief in the v1.32 batch).
5. **Regression test:** `scripts/watchdog-test` (watchdog behaviour). The probes stay on in monitoring.
