# Android review: contract proposal v1.13 (1:1 voice calls)

**Reviewer:** android role, 2026-10-06. **Proposal:** `contract/proposals/2026-10-06-calls-v1.13.md`
(infra: decision 046). Read together with the crypto review (R1 forward distance, R2 one
encrypt lane, R3 binding/freshness, R4 DTLS checks, R5 audio-level extension) and the server review
of the same day. This review adds the app side and doesn't repeat those points.
**Verdict:** feasible, but the biggest risk isn't WebRTC. It's **ringing a phone whose app process
is dead and whose session is fingerprint-locked** (R1). Merge after the required changes R1–R9.

How the current app does things (facts this review relies on):
- `minSdk 26`, `targetSdk 37`, `compileSdk 37`. The release APK is **~21 MB** (nightly.9), with 4
  ABIs (`arm64-v8a`, `armeabi-v7a`, `x86`, `x86_64`), compressed native libs
  (`useLegacyPackaging`) and R8 off.
- Manifest: `INTERNET` and `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` only. There is one FCM service, which handles only
  `{"type":"inbox"}` and enqueues `InboxSyncWorker` (expedited, unique work with **`KEEP`**).
- **The realtime socket is foreground-only.** A push wake-up connects for at most 25 s + 1.5 s
  (`syncAndNotify`), then the `backgroundSync` flag drops and the socket closes.
- **OIDC sessions lock on process death.** The refresh token is sealed by a Keystore RSA key with
  `setUserAuthenticationRequired(true)` and timeout **0** (per-use biometric/credential).
  - Until the user unlocks: no access token, no socket, no MLS.
  - `syncAndNotify` then only posts "Open RisiMe to read them".
- `MlsPipeline` processes e2ee events in order. When an event is parked ahead of the local epoch
  it triggers a commit catch-up **only for `grp:`** conversations; DMs wait for the next sync.
- Notifications: `messages` (HIGH), `requests`, `sync` (MIN) channels. No call channel, no
  `setOnlyAlertOnce`, no full-screen intents.
- `gradle/libs.versions.toml` has no WebRTC and no `core-telecom`.

---

## Required changes (blocking)

**R1. A locked or dead app can't ring. The proposal must pick how a call reaches it.**
§16.8 assumes that "on receipt the app starts its sync at once". On this app, after process death
(normal in Doze, on swipe-away, or under OEM battery managers):
- the sync needs a bearer;
- the bearer needs the refresh token;
- the refresh token needs a fingerprint.

So the device can't fetch, decrypt or ring the offer, and can't fetch TURN credentials either. The
same limitation already hides message notifications, but a call is useless 45 s later. The options:
- **(a) A background device credential.** At unlock, the server issues a long-lived,
  **device-bound, narrowly scoped** token (inbox join/sync, `call:signal`, `GET /calls/turn`, no
  REST writes). It is sealed with a Keystore key that does **not** require user auth (like
  `MlsDbKey`). This weakens decision 014's "fingerprint protects the session" for these scopes
  only, so it needs a root decision and a server change.
- **(b) Ring blind.** On a `call` push while locked, show an incoming-call UI with no name ("RisiMe
  call, unlock to answer"). Unlock → sync → decrypt; if the offer is stale or answered elsewhere,
  end it with "Call ended". This brings false rings and a slow answer.
- **(c) Accept it**, and document that a locked phone only shows the missed call later.

Android recommends **(a)** (a root decision), with (b) as the interim pilot behaviour. Whatever is
chosen goes into §16.8. **Messages would benefit from (a) too.**

**R2. Glare must stop the losing call's rings (§16.5).**
The losing device "drops its outgoing call silently (no `call_end`)". But its `call_offer` has
already reached the **winner's sibling devices**, which ring for up to 45 s for a call that will
never be answered there.
- **Sibling rule (normative).** A device of user U that sees its own user's `call_offer` to X
  (through the sender copy) records "U is calling X" until that call ends. While that holds it
  **doesn't ring** for an offer from X; only the calling device resolves glare.
- **Explicit cancel.** Add an **ephemeral `call_cancel` `{call_id, reason: "glare"}`**, sent with
  `ring: false`. It covers rings that started before the sibling saw its own user's offer. Receivers
  stop ringing and render nothing. Alternatively, use a `call_end` reason `superseded` that is never
  rendered, but an ephemeral signal keeps the history clean.

**R3. Decide to ring only after the whole page is applied (§16.3, §16.4).**
A device that comes online during a call gets the offer **and** the later `call_answer` (sibling
copy), `call_accepted`, `call_cancel` or `call_end` in the same join or sync page. Ringing per
event would ring and stop at once, or ring for a call that's already over. Normative: "a device
rings only after it has applied all events of the page that contained the offer, and only if its
call state is still *incoming*". The live pushes after a join follow the per-event rule.

**R4. Missing timer: an answering device waiting for `call_accepted` (§16.4 table).**
Callee device B answers, but the caller picks sibling C (whose answer came first), or the caller's
`call_accepted` is lost. B then waits forever in "Connecting…". Add: *Accept wait*, **10 s** after
sending `call_answer` without a `call_accepted` naming this device → tear down quietly with
"Answered on another device" (if a sibling answer was seen) or "Call ended", and send no
`call_end`.

**R5. Correct §16.9 on the microphone foreground service and CallStyle (Android 12/14+ rules).**
- **Answer launches an activity.** A `microphone` FGS can't be started from the background on
  Android 14+ (while-in-use permission), and an Answer action that is a broadcast or service
  `PendingIntent` doesn't count as "visible". The **Answer action is a `PendingIntent.getActivity`**
  to the in-call activity (`showWhenLocked`, `turnScreenOn`; on API 26 the window flags
  `FLAG_SHOW_WHEN_LOCKED | FLAG_TURN_SCREEN_ON`). That activity checks `RECORD_AUDIO`, then starts
  or upgrades the FGS to `phoneCall|microphone`.
- **Ringing uses the `phoneCall` type only.** Its prerequisite is `MANAGE_OWN_CALLS`, which allows
  starting it from the high-priority FCM window. The FGS also keeps the socket alive (R6).
- **CallStyle needs a full-screen intent or an FGS.** Since API 31, a CallStyle notification must
  have a full-screen intent or belong to a foreground service, otherwise `IllegalArgumentException`.
  - Incoming: `CallStyle.forIncomingCall` **always sets the full-screen intent**; the system turns
    it into a heads-up when FSI isn't allowed.
  - Ongoing: `forOngoingCall` is the FGS notification.
- **Start the FGS with the microphone type only after `RECORD_AUDIO` is granted**, otherwise
  `startForeground` throws a `SecurityException` on 14+.

**R6. The call holds the socket, and the call push has its own wake-up path (§16.8).**
- **The socket stays up.** While a call is ringing, connecting or active, the realtime connection
  stays up regardless of `foreground` (`combine(foreground, backgroundSync, callActive)`), and a
  partial wake lock is held (released at end, capped at the 4-h max). Otherwise the
  25-s background sync closes the socket in the middle of ringing and the device never sees the
  `call_answer`, `call_accepted` or `call_ice`.
- **A separate wake-up.** The `call` push gets its own handler: start the ringing FGS (phoneCall)
  at once from `onMessageReceived` and sync inside it. It must not go through
  `InboxSyncWorker`'s `KEEP` unique work, which can swallow it behind a running inbox sync, and
  expedited jobs can be deferred by quota.
- **Spec wording.** §16.8 says "high-priority FCM grants the background start". Add: "of a
  foreground service, which the client starts immediately".

**R7. Calls in ordered processing: the DM catch-up, and silence for control kinds (§16.3, A4).**
Agree with crypto R2: no "decrypt call signals first" fast path. Two client rules make the
ordered path fast and quiet:
- A `call_signal` (or any e2ee event) parked ahead of the local epoch in a **DM** triggers an
  immediate commit catch-up, as groups already do. Today DMs wait for the next sync, which can be
  minutes after a push wake-up.
- A `call_signal` that is pre-install, undecryptable, expired or binding-failed (crypto R3)
  creates **no** §13.3 "couldn't be decrypted" line and no history marker. It is logged only.
  The same rule as in the delete review R5; state it once for every control kind.

**R8. One lane per conversation for encrypt + push (crypto R2), on Android concretely.**
Today texts and reactions encrypt under two different mutexes, and calls add a third sender with
bursts of up to 60 per 10 s. One per-conversation lane, a `Mutex` keyed by `conversation_id` shared
by the outbox, reactions, deletes and `call:signal`, is required before the first call ships. ICE
batches waiting behind a stuck text push must not stall: a text that gets `Unavailable` gives up
the lane, and is re-encrypted when it retries.

**R9. Busy and cellular calls without `READ_PHONE_STATE` (§16.5, A6).**
§16.5 says "in a cellular call reported by Telecom". `TelecomManager.isInCall()` needs
`READ_PHONE_STATE`, which we shouldn't ask for. Use `AudioManager.mode` in
{`MODE_IN_CALL`, `MODE_IN_COMMUNICATION`} (no permission) as "busy". Telecom also tells the
self-managed call when a cellular call takes over (`onSetInactive`/disconnect). Adjust the
sentence to "busy if the device is in another call (own call state, or the audio mode says a call
is active)".

## Suggestions

- **S-a. Send the offer immediately (A5).** Host candidates are ready within milliseconds, so
  don't hold the offer for ~1 s. Then send `call_ice` batches every 250 ms. The callee needs the
  caller's candidates only after a human taps Answer (seconds later), so trickle costs no
  latency. On ring, the callee prefetches TURN credentials but allocates nothing until Answer
  (coturn `user-quota`).
- **S-b. Ringtone through the channel.** Create a dedicated `calls` channel at IMPORTANCE_HIGH,
  with the default ringtone (`USAGE_NOTIFICATION_RINGTONE`) and a vibration pattern, and set
  `Notification.FLAG_INSISTENT` on the incoming notification so it loops until cancelled. This
  respects ringer mode, DND and per-channel user settings without our own `MediaPlayer` (answers
  A7). Put a `Person` on the CallStyle so "calls from contacts" DND rules can apply.
- **S-c. Audio focus and mode belong to Telecom.** With `core-telecom`, don't call
  `requestAudioFocus`, `setMode` or `setSpeakerphoneOn`. Route only through
  `requestEndpointChange`. WebRTC's `JavaAudioDeviceModule` uses `USAGE_VOICE_COMMUNICATION`, with
  hardware AEC/NS when `isAvailable()`.
- **S-d. Proximity.** Hold `PROXIMITY_SCREEN_OFF_WAKE_LOCK` only while the current endpoint is
  `TYPE_EARPIECE` and the call is active; release it on speaker, wired or Bluetooth.
- **S-e. Bluetooth.** Telecom routes a connected headset automatically on answer, and endpoint
  names come from `CallEndpointCompat`. **Don't request `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`** until a pilot phone
  shows unnamed endpoints. That's one less runtime prompt.
- **S-f. `calls` advertised only when it can work:** WebRTC's native library loaded, core-telecom
  registered, and `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` granted. Settings shows a "Calls" row explaining what's
  missing (notifications, full-screen permission, battery optimisation).
- **S-g. Debug stats.** A debug-only overlay with `getStats()`: the candidate pair type
  (host/srflx/relay), RTT, loss, jitter and codec. It's indispensable for the real-network tests
  below. Never log IPs in release builds.
- **S-h. Store `call_id` in its own column** on the call-history rows (`kind = 'call'`), with an
  index on `(conversation_id, call_id)`. "One line per `call_id`" (§16.2) is then a cheap lookup.
  Call lines are system rows: "Delete for me" only (consistent with the v1.12 review).
- **S-i. Licences screen.** Add Settings → "Open-source licences", listing libwebrtc
  (BSD-3-Clause + PATENTS) and its bundled components (Opus, libsrtp, BoringSSL, abseil, libyuv,
  …), core-telecom (Apache-2.0) and the existing libraries. The APK is distributed outside Play,
  so the notices are ours to ship.

## Answers to the android open points

**A1. WebRTC build: use `io.github.webrtc-sdk:android-prefixed`, at the version LiveKit pins.**
Checked on Maven Central (2026-10-06):

| | `io.getstream:stream-webrtc-android` 1.3.10 | `io.github.webrtc-sdk:android-prefixed` 144.7559.14 (pinned by `io.livekit:livekit-android` 2.29.0) |
|---|---|---|
| Java package / native lib | `org.webrtc` / `libjingle_peerconnection_so.so` | `livekit.org.webrtc` / `liblkjingle_peerconnection_so.so` (shaded, `libs/shadow-all.jar`) |
| ABIs | arm64-v8a, armeabi-v7a, x86, x86_64 | the same four |
| arm64-v8a .so (raw / deflated) | 11.5 MB / 5.4 MB | 12.1 MB / 5.7 MB |
| x86_64 | 12.8 / 5.4 MB | 16.1 / 6.8 MB |
| armeabi-v7a + x86 | 6.6 + 12.3 MB / 3.9 + 5.3 MB | 6.8 + 12.6 MB / 4.1 + 5.5 MB |
| Licence | Apache-2.0 wrapper + BSD-3 libwebrtc | BSD-3-Clause (libwebrtc fork) |
| Extras | — | `FrameCryptor` (the insertable-streams E2EE that v1.14 needs) |

- **They can coexist** (different packages and .so names), but that means **two** libwebrtc copies,
  about +22 MB compressed per copy across 4 ABIs. Using LiveKit's artifact for 1:1 now means v1.14
  adds only the LiveKit SDK (Kotlin) on top of the same native library.
- **Pin it** in `libs.versions.toml` with a comment "must equal livekit-android's pom", and add a
  Gradle check that fails if two `*jingle_peerconnection_so.so` files end up in the APK.
- **Use LiveKit's SDK for groups only.** Don't use it for 1:1 now: it only does SFU rooms, and its
  extra dependencies buy nothing for P2P.
- Version 150.7871.01 exists too. Stay on LiveKit's pin, not on the newest.
- The library is a prebuilt AAR, so nothing needs building on spark2. JVM and Robolectric tests
  can't load the native library: put it behind a `CallMedia` interface with a fake in tests.

**APK size.** Adding it as is takes the release APK from ~21 to **~43 MB**. Recommended:
- exclude the WebRTC `.so` for `armeabi-v7a` and `x86` via `packaging.jniLibs.excludes`, and keep
  the MLS core on all four ABIs: **+12.5 MB** (arm64 and the x86_64 emulator);
- advertise `calls` only if `System.loadLibrary` succeeds (S-f), so a 32-bit phone just shows
  "Calls aren't supported on this phone";
- per-ABI APKs in the updater (`version.json` with per-ABI URLs) would cut it to +5.7 MB, but they
  need a decision-016 amendment: later, if size hurts.
- Check whether any pilot phone is 32-bit before excluding v7a.

**A2. Use `androidx.core:core-telecom` 1.0.1 (stable), not a hand-written `ConnectionService`.**
It is the Google-maintained path:
- `CallsManager.registerAppWithTelecom` and `addCall`;
- ConnectionService underneath on API 26–33, transactional Telecom on 34+;
- `availableEndpoints`, `currentCallEndpoint` and `requestEndpointChange` (CallEndpointCompat);
- min SDK 26.

The 1.1 betas add call extensions we don't need. OEM behaviour must be checked on the pilot
phones:
- **Xiaomi/HyperOS** needs the extra "Show on lock screen" and "Display pop-up windows while
  running in background" toggles for full-screen intents, and its autostart blocks FCM after a
  swipe-away.
- **Samsung** handles self-managed calls well, but "Sleeping apps" delays FCM.

The Settings "Calls" row (S-f) deep-links to what's missing.

**A3. `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT`.**
- **Expected behaviour.** On 14+ it's an app-op that Play revokes at install for non-calling apps.
  Our installs come from the browser or our own `PackageInstaller` session, so the expected
  default is **granted**.
- **Check every time.** Check `canUseFullScreenIntent()` at sign-in and before **every** incoming
  ring (the user can revoke it). If it's false, ring as an insistent heads-up (S-b), and
  afterwards show one in-app card that opens `ACTION_MANAGE_APP_USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT`.
- **Verify** on the pilot phones on 14, 15 and 16, from both the browser install and an updater
  install.

**A4.** No fast path (crypto R2). Make the ordered path fast instead: DM catch-up on park (R7);
the R6 wake-up path, which starts work at once; and processing the join page in one pass.

**A5.** Use full trickle with an immediate offer (S-a). Ring latency is dominated by push and
sync, not candidates.

**A6.** Busy uses own state plus the audio-mode heuristic (R9). Call waiting comes later:
core-telecom supports holding (`setInactive`), so v1.14+ can add it without new permissions.

**A7.**
- Ringtone, vibration and DND go through the channel (S-b).
- Audio focus is Telecom's (S-c).
- Bluetooth is automatic through Telecom (S-e).
- Speaker is never the default for voice.
- A wired headset wins over the earpiece.

**A8.**
- **Keep the offset.** Keep the clock offset from every join/sync reply (the delete review R8 needs
  it too). A pushed wake-up always gets a fresh `server_time` in its join reply before the page's
  events, so a woken device never rings on a stale offset.
- **Bound live pushes.** Live pushes later in the session use the stored offset. Crypto R3's
  `sent_at` bounds both clocks.
- **Server TTL.** It caps staleness: a ring signal is gone after 60 s anyway.

**Other points the review was asked to check:**
- **Permissions (manifest):**
  - `RECORD_AUDIO` (runtime: on the first outgoing call, or in the in-call activity on the first
    answer);
  - `MANAGE_OWN_CALLS`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_PHONE_CALL`,
    `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE`, `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT`, `WAKE_LOCK`,
    `MODIFY_AUDIO_SETTINGS` and `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` (the last two needed by WebRTC);
  - optionally `REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS`, only if the pilot shows missed rings (we're
    not on Play).

  Not needed: `READ_PHONE_STATE`, `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` (S-e), `CAMERA` (no video in v1.13).
- **High-priority FCM in Doze:**
  - **Delivery.** High-priority data messages are delivered in Doze and grant a short exemption to
    start an FGS (R6).
  - **Deprioritisation.** FCM deprioritises high-priority messages that don't lead to a
    user-visible notification. Stale or answered-elsewhere offers produce none, so keep those rare
    and never send `ring: true` for restarts (already specified).
  - **Restricted bucket.** Apps in the *restricted* standby bucket (rarely opened, or OEM
    "deep sleep") may lose messages. That's the Settings hint above.
- **Multi-device state machine** (pure Kotlin, JVM-tested):
  - States: idle → outgoing(calling/ringing) | incoming(ringing) → answering → connecting → active
    → ended.
  - Events: the 7 envelope types, `call_cancel` (R2), the timers (45 s ring, 10 s accept wait R4,
    20 s connect, 15 s restart, 4 h max), Telecom callbacks and network changes.
  - Per call, record the selected device pair, the offer's device (crypto R3 pinning) and
    "rang on this device" (for the "Missed" vs "Couldn't connect" line).
  - Glare on the calling device, plus the sibling rule (R2).

## Testing on real networks
spark2's TURN ports are still filtered (decision 046 port check), so nothing beyond the same LAN
works until they're open. Then:
1. **Same Wi-Fi** (host candidates): emulator (x86_64, host-audio mic on) ↔ USB phone, then
   phone ↔ phone.
2. **Relay forced:** both devices with `iceTransportPolicy = relay` (debug toggle). This is the
   §16.13 interop case and proves coturn, the credentials and the quotas.
3. **Wi-Fi ↔ mobile data**, then **mobile ↔ mobile on two different carriers**: CGNAT/symmetric
   NAT, so expect relay. Record the candidate-pair type from S-g.
4. **UDP-hostile Wi-Fi** (a corporate guest network or a router rule): TURN over TCP 3478, and
   TURNS 5349 once TLS is live.
5. **Handover:** walk out of Wi-Fi in the middle of a call → ICE restart within 15 s.
6. **Doze/killed/locked:**
   - `adb shell dumpsys deviceidle force-idle`;
   - swipe-away;
   - the screen locked for 30+ min;
   - after a reboot.

   Each must ring, or behave as R1 decides.
7. **Audio routing:** earpiece and proximity, speaker, wired headset, Bluetooth headset connected
   before and during the call, a cellular call arriving during a RisiMe call (R9).
8. **Impairment:** `tc netem` on the laptop hotspot (5 % loss, 200 ms delay, jitter) for Opus FEC.

The gates stay JVM-only on spark2: state machine, envelopes, the binding and freshness checks, and
the SDP validation, all with a fake `CallMedia`. Redroid (decision 045) has no audio HAL, so the
real media test is the laptop/phone interop run. It isn't a gate until it can run unattended.

## Chunk order (android)
0. **Decisions and prerequisites:**
   - R1 (background credential) decided;
   - TURN ports open (decision 046);
   - the core's `maximum_forward_distance` and tolerance (crypto R1/R2);
   - the per-conversation lane (R8, shared with v1.12);
   - pilot phones checked for 32-bit.
1. **Protocol + state machine (JVM only):**
   - envelopes (encode, decode, strict and SDP validation per crypto R4–R6, `sent_at`,
     `call_cancel`);
   - `call:signal` push and reply, the `call_signal` event, `GET /calls/turn`, the `call` push
     type;
   - the clock offset;
   - `CallStateMachine` with every transition from R2–R4 and §16.4–16.5.
2. **Pipeline and history:**
   - `call_signal` through `MlsPipeline` in order, with the binding checks;
   - no markers (R7) and DM catch-up on park;
   - the page-level ring decision (R3);
   - `call_end` rows (`kind = 'call'`, `call_id` column, Room migration) and history lines in both
     perspectives;
   - preview, missed-call notification, no notifications on replay.

   `calls` is not advertised yet.
3. **WebRTC:**
   - `android-prefixed` pinned to LiveKit's version, the ABI excludes, the duplicate-lib check;
   - the `CallMedia` wrapper;
   - Opus parameters, stripping the audio-level extension (crypto R5);
   - the DTLS assertions (crypto R4e);
   - the licences screen (S-i).
4. **Telecom + UI:**
   - core-telecom registration and `addCall`;
   - the call FGS (phoneCall → +microphone, R5);
   - the `calls` channel and the insistent CallStyle (S-b);
   - the incoming full-screen activity (lock screen, API 26 flags);
   - the in-call screen (mute, endpoint picker, end, timer), proximity, wake locks;
   - the permission flows (`RECORD_AUDIO`, FSI settings card).
5. **Wake-up and background (R6):**
   - the `call` push handler;
   - the call-scoped socket;
   - R1's chosen behaviour for a locked app;
   - Doze and killed-app tests on the pilot phones.
6. **Enable:**
   - advertise `calls` (S-f);
   - `calls_ready`/`missing_calls` button states, the call button in the DM header, "Call back" on
     history lines;
   - interop on the same LAN, then relay-only through coturn (§16.13), including the negative
     fingerprint test (crypto R4f).
7. **Real-network matrix** (above) on the pilot phones. Then fix, then `READY` in
   `docs/status/android.md`.

v1.14 (groups) then adds `io.livekit:livekit-android` on the same native library, with
`FrameCryptor` keyed from the MLS exporter.
