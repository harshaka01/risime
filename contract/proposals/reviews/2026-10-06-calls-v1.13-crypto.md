# Crypto review: contract proposal v1.13 (voice calls), crypto parts

**Reviewer:** crypto role, 2026-10-06. **Proposal:** `contract/proposals/2026-10-06-calls-v1.13.md`
(§16.0, §16.2, §16.3, §16.10, §16.12), decision 046. Read against PROTOCOL v1.11 §10, §12, §14 and
`crypto/risime-mls{,-ffi}` (OpenMLS 0.9.0). Server-only and Android-only points (rate limits, Telecom,
FCM) are left to those reviews except where they touch the crypto.

**Verdict:** the core idea is right and standard. Signalling travels inside MLS, so the SDP's
`a=fingerprint` is authenticated by the sender's attested leaf, which makes DTLS-SRTP
authenticated end to end, also through TURN. Merge after **R1–R6**. The two that matter most:
- R1: generation gaps from expiring signals can exceed OpenMLS's forward window and make *later
  chat messages* undecryptable;
- R4: the app must prove that libwebrtc enforces the fingerprint and that encryption can't be
  switched off.

---

## Confirmed: the DTLS-SRTP chain of trust
1. The offerer's libwebrtc generates a DTLS certificate. Its SHA-256 fingerprint goes into the
   offer SDP (`a=fingerprint:sha-256 …`).
2. The SDP travels in an MLS PrivateMessage. It is signed by the sender's leaf key (Ed25519) and
   encrypted under the epoch's keys. The leaf's identity `"<user_id>/<device_id>"` and key are
   bound by the server's attestation and checked offline in the core (§10.0). The core verifies
   signature and AEAD and returns the sender's identity (`Incoming::Application.sender`).
3. The answerer passes that SDP to `setRemoteDescription`. During the DTLS handshake libwebrtc
   checks the peer's certificate against the remote description's fingerprint and fails the
   transport on a mismatch. Note that it does **not** check the CA chain; the fingerprint is the
   whole check.
4. The same holds in reverse for the answer.

So a relay (coturn), the RisiMe server or the network can forward or drop packets, but can't
terminate DTLS on either side without a key whose fingerprint was signed into MLS by the peer's
device. **The claim in §16.0/§16.10 holds.** It holds only if the app never feeds an SDP from any
other path into the peer connection and never disables encryption. R4 makes the app verify both.

The binding is to *the peer device's WebRTC stack for this call*, not to a long-term key. That is
the right granularity (a fresh certificate per call, R4c).

## Required changes (blocking)

**R1. Expiring signals create generation gaps; bound them (shared with the delete review, R5).**

Every `call_*` envelope consumes a generation of the sender leaf's application ratchet in the DM
group. A device that never receives a signal skips that generation:
- the signal expired before it synced (TTL 60/120 s);
- the device lacks `calls` (the §16.1 delivery filter removes signals from its sockets);
- the device is a pre-v1.13 app.

OpenMLS (`SenderRatchetConfiguration` default `(5, 1000)`, which the core uses) refuses a message
more than **1000 generations** ahead of the receiver's head for that sender
(`TooDistantInTheFuture` → `DecryptionFailed`). The gap that counts is the one between two
messages *this device receives from that leaf*, so it accumulates across calls.
- Example: the callee's phone sends `call_ringing`, `call_answer` and `call_ice` batches (5–10
  ephemeral messages per call).
- If the caller usually hangs up, the callee's leaf sends no durable message at all.
- After ~100–200 answered calls without a text, the caller's mostly-offline tablet (or a device
  without `calls`) can't decrypt the callee's next **chat message**.
- DM epochs change rarely, so the counter doesn't reset.

§16.3's sentence "the gap is within MLS's forward tolerance (≤ 1000 generations)" is therefore
not true in general. Fix:
- Core: `maximum_forward_distance` 20 000 in every group config, and migrate stored groups on load
  (delete review R5; one core change serves both).
- Contract: replace the §16.3 sentence with "Expired or filtered signals leave gaps in the
  sender's ratchet; the core tolerates gaps up to 20 000 generations between two received messages
  of one sender device."
- Android test: 2 000 expired signals, then a text that still decrypts on the device that missed
  them.

**R2. One ordered encrypt-and-send lane per group (out-of-order tolerance).**

Signals are encrypted on the call path; texts are encrypted in the outbox. Both use the same leaf
ratchet. With `out_of_order_tolerance` 5, a receiver that gets generation `n+10` before `n` loses
`n` for good. That happens if a text encrypted at `n` is pushed after a burst of 10 ICE batches.
- **Normative (client):** encryption and the channel push for one group go through one serial
  lane. A ciphertext that wasn't accepted before a later one from this device was pushed is
  **re-encrypted**, never resent (`client_msg_id`/`call_id` stay the same).
- Phoenix channel pushes from one socket are processed in order on the server, so the lane is
  enough.
- Core: raise `out_of_order_tolerance` to 32 as a margin. That keeps ≤ 32 message keys per sender
  per epoch; the forward-secrecy cost is accepted.

**This also answers A4: no "decrypt call signals first" fast path.** Decrypting a later generation
first discards the keys of more than `out_of_order_tolerance` earlier pending messages of that
sender. A fast path would silently destroy chat messages. Keep the strict order and make catch-up
fast instead: fetch commits first, then process.

**R3. Bind the cleartext routing fields to the encrypted envelope, and check freshness inside MLS.**

The server sees `call_id` and `ring` in cleartext and the device rings on the decrypted envelope.
These two must agree, and the device must not trust the server's clock alone.
- **Binding** (receiver, before any action): `envelope.call_id == event.call_id`. Also
  `event.ring == (envelope.type == "call_offer" && !envelope.restart)`. Then
  `event.from`/`from_device` == the core's `sender`, as in §10.3. On any mismatch, drop and log.
  A server that relabels an ICE batch as `ring: true` (forcing a high-priority push) or swaps
  `call_id`s across calls is caught.
- **Freshness:** the server controls `server_ts` and `server_time`, so a malicious or broken
  server can replay *an offer this device never decrypted* (held back within the ratchet window)
  and make it ring long after the call. Add **`"sent_at"`** (the sender's **server-corrected**
  time, RFC 3339 ms) to `call_offer` (also with `restart: true`). The callee rings only if both
  hold:
  - `|now_corrected − sent_at| < 45 s`;
  - the existing `server_time − server_ts < 45 s`.
- **Dedupe:** each device keeps the `call_id`s it has acted on for 24 h and never rings or answers
  the same `call_id` twice. MLS already prevents byte replay on one device (consumed keys); this
  covers re-encrypted resends after `stale_epoch`.
- **Per-call sender pinning** (cheap, prevents sibling-device confusion): for a `call_id`, record
  the offer's sender device `C`.
  - Callee devices accept `call_accepted`, `call_ice` and restarts for that call only from `C`.
  - The caller accepts `call_answer` only from a device of the peer user, and after
    `call_accepted` only from the selected device `A`.
  - `call_end` is accepted from either user (both are DM members; it is display-only).

  Signals from other devices are ignored, not errors.

**R4. The app must verify that DTLS is enforced. Don't just assume it.**

Make these normative in §16.9/§16.10 and test them in the Android gate:
- (a) **The remote SDP comes only from a decrypted MLS envelope** whose sender passed R3. There is
  no other `setRemoteDescription` source: no debug intent, no server field, no deep link.
- (b) **Encryption can't be off:** `PeerConnectionFactory.Options.disableEncryption` stays
  `false`. The stream-webrtc build exposes it; add a unit test that reads the options the factory
  was built with. Strict validation (§16.2) also **rejects** SDP containing `a=crypto` (SDES) or
  lacking `a=fingerprint`, and rejects a fingerprint algorithm other than `sha-256`. Multiple
  `a=fingerprint` lines are rejected too (one certificate per call).
- (c) **A fresh certificate per call:** let libwebrtc generate it per `RTCPeerConnection` (ECDSA
  P-256), and never persist or reuse an `RTCCertificate` across calls. That prevents linking calls
  by the fingerprint.
- (d) **Pin the fingerprint for the whole call:** a `restart: true` offer, or any renegotiation,
  must carry the **same** `a=fingerprint` as the first SDP from that peer device. A different one
  means tearing down the call (`call_end` `failed`). Restarts change ICE credentials, not DTLS
  identity.
- (e) **Post-connect assertion:** after ICE `connected`, read `getStats()` and require:
  - `transport.dtlsState == "connected"`;
  - a non-empty `srtpCipher`;
  - `remoteCertificateId` → `RTCCertificateStats.fingerprint` equal (case-insensitively, colons
    ignored) to the fingerprint in the MLS-delivered SDP.

  On failure, tear down with `failed` and log `dtls_fingerprint_mismatch`. libwebrtc already
  enforces this. The assertion makes the guarantee testable, and it catches a future library
  swap (A1) that behaves differently.
- (f) **Negative interop test:** flip one byte of the fingerprint in the answer SDP before
  `setRemoteDescription` (test hook in debug builds only) → the call must fail to connect. Then
  run a relay-only call (`iceTransportPolicy = relay`) through coturn and check (e) again.

**R5. RTP header extensions leak voice activity through TURN.**

SRTP encrypts the RTP payload, not the RTP header extensions. libwebrtc's default audio offer
negotiates `urn:ietf:params:rtp-hdrext:ssrc-audio-level` (RFC 6464): a per-packet speech level
readable by coturn and by any on-path observer. Either:
- strip that extmap from the offer and answer (simple; the 1:1 call doesn't need it), or
- enable encrypted header extensions (`CryptoOptions.SRtp.enableEncryptedRtpHeaderExtensions`, or
  cryptex where both ends support it).

Strip it in v1.13. Strict validation then rejects an SDP that still carries it.

**R6. Strict validation covers the crypto-relevant SDP lines too (§16.2).**

Besides the m-line checks:
- `a=setup` must be `actpass` (offer) or `active`/`passive` (answer);
- `a=ice-ufrag`/`a=ice-pwd` must be present;
- no `a=crypto`;
- exactly one `a=fingerprint:sha-256`, with 32 colon-separated hex bytes;
- `a=rtcp-mux` must be present.

Reject before ringing (offer) or before applying (answer). That keeps parser bugs in libwebrtc
behind a strict front door for content that a peer, a friend but still a remote party, controls.

## Suggestions (non-blocking)

- **S1. Opus CBR.** VBR packet sizes on an encrypted voice stream leak spoken content to an
  on-path observer (phrase spotting on VBR VoIP is published work). At 32 kbit/s,
  `cbr=1` costs almost nothing. DTX still reveals talk/silence timing. That is accepted and
  should be written into §16.10's "coturn learns" list.
- **S2. SRTP suite:** prefer `AEAD_AES_128_GCM` (`CryptoOptions.SRtp.enableGcmCryptoSuites =
  true`), with `AES_CM_128_HMAC_SHA1_80` as a fallback. Test (e) logs which one was negotiated.
- **S3. Size and latency are fine.** The MLS cost per signal is one Ed25519 signature, one
  verification and one AES-128-GCM pass, well under 1 ms on the pilot phones. The overhead is
  ≈ 150–250 bytes (header, group id, signature, tag) plus `padding_size` (0 today). So the
  20 480-byte envelope cap fits the 24 KiB ciphertext cap with room to spare. Latency is
  dominated by sync and in-order catch-up (R2), not by crypto. Half-trickle (A5) is a network
  trade-off with no crypto impact, except that each extra `call_ice` costs a generation (R1).
- **S4. `call_end` fields are sender claims.** `connected_at`, `duration_s` and `reason` are
  authenticated as "this device said so", not as facts. The history line may show them as they
  are. Never use them for anything that matters (billing, abuse decisions).
- **S5. Safety-number display (later).** The call UI can show "End-to-end encrypted" once (e)
  passes. A user-visible code derived from both DTLS fingerprints and the MLS
  `epoch_authenticator` would add nothing against the server (MLS already covers it), so skip it.
- **S6. Push hint.** `{"type":"call"}` reveals the call/message distinction to FCM (§16.8). This
  is accepted as written; there are no crypto objections.

## v1.14 LiveKit outline (§16.12): comments for the future proposal

The direction is right: frame encryption keyed from the MLS exporter, so the SFU only forwards
ciphertext. The outline needs these changes before it becomes normative:

1. **Per-sender keys, not one shared key (nonce safety).** LiveKit's frame cryptor uses
   AES-GCM with an IV derived from SSRC, timestamp and a counter. One key shared by up to 32
   senders makes IV uniqueness depend on SSRC uniqueness, which nobody guarantees across
   renegotiations. Follow RFC 9605 (SFrame) §5.2, adapted:
   ```
   call_epoch_secret  = MLS-Exporter("risime-call-v1", call_id_bytes16, 32)   # per epoch
   sender_base_key[i] = HKDF-Expand(call_epoch_secret, "risime-call-v1 sender" || uint32_be(leaf_index i), 32)
   ```
   and give it to LiveKit's **per-participant** key provider (`sharedKey = false`), keyed by the
   participant whose LiveKit identity `<user_id>/<device_id>` maps to that MLS leaf.
   - Per-sender keys **don't authenticate senders among members**: every member can derive every
     key, so any member could forge another member's audio. State that as a known limit.
     Per-frame signatures are out of scope.
   - What they do give: nonce separation, and the guarantee that the SFU and outsiders can neither
     read nor forge frames.
   - `call_id_bytes16` is the 16 raw UUID bytes, not the string. Fix the encoding in the
     contract.
2. **Key index = epoch, and a keyring across the switch.** Use LiveKit's `keyIndex` = `epoch mod
   16` and keep the previous epoch's keys for a short grace period (≤ 10 s) for decryption only.
   - Senders switch to the new key as soon as their device merges the commit.
   - Receivers try the frame's key index.

   Devices merge a commit at different times, so without a keyring every epoch change drops audio
   for a moment.
3. **The core exports at merge time.** `MlsGroup::export_secret` works only for the **current**
   epoch; past epochs can't be exported later. Add an FFI
   `export_secret(gid, label, context, len)`. When a call is active, the app derives the next
   keys **in the same handler that processed the commit**, before the next frame.
   - Catch-up through `process_commits` jumps several epochs; intermediate epochs' keys are never
     needed, because no one encrypts under an epoch that has already passed for the whole group.
     Senders that are behind are caught by the grace period above.
   - Alternative: the core returns the exporter output with each `Incoming::Commit` for groups
     flagged "in call". That is cleaner, but needs a "call active" flag in the core. Decide in
     v1.14.
4. **Removal is cryptographic *and* administrative.** A removal commit gives a new epoch and new
   keys, so the removed device can't decrypt later frames. During the grace period, though, it
   can still decrypt frames from members who haven't merged yet.
   - The server must also revoke that participant's LiveKit access (kick from the room) the
     moment the removal commit lands.
   - Room tokens should have short TTLs (≤ 10 min, refreshed) so a removed member can't reconnect.
5. **Roster from MLS, not from LiveKit.** The SFU (operated by the server) can admit a participant
   that isn't an MLS member. It can't decrypt, but it would *appear* in the call.
   - The call UI lists only participants whose LiveKit identity maps to a **current MLS leaf**
     and who announced themselves with an MLS-authenticated join envelope.
   - A LiveKit participant with no matching leaf is never shown and never gets a key.
6. **Fail closed.** The publisher refuses to publish until its key is set. Receivers drop frames
   that fail decryption; there is never a fallback to unencrypted frames. If LiveKit's "failure
   tolerance" exists, set it so decryption failures are never played.
7. **Exporter labels are domain-separated per use.** Use `risime-call-v1` (calls) only for this;
   image keys, delete AAD and any future exporter use get their own labels. Write a short label
   registry into §10.
8. **Joiners mid-call** get keys only from their add epoch onwards. That is correct, and it means
   an "add to call" invitation can't let them hear audio from before they joined. Note it in the
   UI copy only if Harsha wants it ("you'll hear the call from now on").

## Answers
- **Signalling inside MLS (size, latency):** fine. See S3. The real costs are generation
  consumption (R1) and the per-leaf ordering constraint (R2), not bytes or CPU.
- **Replay of stale offers:**
  - byte replay to the same device fails (the key is consumed);
  - a held-back, never-delivered offer can still ring today, which R3 (`sent_at` + dedupe)
    closes;
  - replay into another conversation fails the core's group-id check.
- **Binding `call_id`:** cleartext `call_id`/`ring` must equal the envelope (R3). Per-call sender
  pinning keeps sibling devices and stale calls out.
- **SDP/ICE confidentiality:** SDP, candidates and IPs are inside MLS. The server sees only
  ciphertext, `call_id`, `ring` and timing (§16.10 is accurate). coturn and the peer still learn
  IPs (accepted, §16.10). RTP header extensions leaked voice activity until R5.
- **DTLS-SRTP fingerprints:** confirmed end-to-end authenticated through MLS (section above). The
  app verifies it under R4 (one SDP source, encryption never disabled, a per-call certificate, a
  pinned fingerprint, the `getStats` assertion and a negative test).
- **A4 (fast path):** no. It would discard keys of pending chat messages (R2).
- **v1.14 LiveKit:** viable. Make the frame keys per sender, derived from the MLS exporter with
  binary `call_id` context, use key index = epoch with a short keyring, have the core export at
  merge, and revoke SFU access on removal. Build the roster from MLS and fail closed.

| Addition | For | Version |
|---|---|---|
| `maximum_forward_distance` 20 000 and `out_of_order_tolerance` 32 in all group configs, plus a migration of stored groups on load | R1, R2 | v1.13 (shared with v1.12 R5) |
